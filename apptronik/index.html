The real reason

Why are we sending cake to Apptronik?

Because of one line in the Robot Park announcement. Not the 90,000 square feet in Austin — the part after it: a growing global network of Robot Parks at customer and partner sites around the world. That sentence is a manufacturing plan to you. To us it reads as a distributed data-ingest architecture, and it's the reason we picked your building.

Apollo 2 has been running at Robot Park for over a year now, in both the bipedal and wheeled-base configurations you showed at Automate. Teleoperation and autonomous execution, continuously, across logistics, manufacturing and retail tasks. That is not a robot demo — it is a data-generation plant, and the output has to physically reach model training, some of it into Gemini Robotics on Google DeepMind's side, some of it into whatever GPUs you're renting for your own runs. Every additional Robot Park at a Mercedes-Benz or GXO site multiplies that flow and adds a new origin point to it.

Fleet scale-out is a networking event before it's a manufacturing event. Each new Robot Park is a new ingest point that has to reach your training stack privately, predictably, and without a 90-day circuit order.

At three sites you can survive shipping that over the public internet and hoping. At thirty — which is roughly where "network of Robot Parks" lands you before Apollo 3 ships commercially in 2027 — every new site becomes a new ingest path, a new cloud on-ramp, a new egress bill, and a new place for jitter to quietly eat GPU utilization you already paid for. With $935M of Series A behind the ramp, the expensive version of this problem is the one where nobody solved the cheap version first.

That's the whole reason for the cake. We'd rather have this conversation while it's still an architecture question instead of an incident channel.

What Megaport actually does

01 / Private lanes, not the public internet

Every Robot Park gets a private on-ramp to every cloud.

One software-defined fabric reaching more than 1,100 data centres across 185+ cities and 30 countries. A virtual cross connect comes up in minutes, not a quarter. Capture leaves a factory floor and arrives in your cloud without ever touching the open internet — the difference between a p99 you can plan against and one you find out about.
